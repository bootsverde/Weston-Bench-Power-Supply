Single-Board 120VAC Ohms Supply

Schematic and build layout for replacing the Weston 785's 1.5V and 15V ohmmeter batteries with one self-contained mains-to-DC board — no separate converter unit. A small isolated AC–DC module does the mains isolation, and its single 24V output feeds two LM317 regulators directly.

Bring-up order

  1. With the case open and the meter's original battery wiring disconnected, wire the mains section (cord, fuse, switch, chassis bond, module L/N) first and verify continuity before connecting anything to the module's DC output.
  2. Power up, confirm ~24V at the module's output, then adjust each LM317's trimmer while reading its output on a multimeter: 15.00V on the top rail, 1.500V on the bottom rail.
  3. Power off. Wire the two regulator outputs and the common bus to the meter's original battery-position wiring, matching the polarity you photographed beforehand.
  4. Power up again, re-check both voltages at the terminal blocks, then check chassis-to-ground continuity (≈0Ω) and hot-to-chassis isolation (open) with the meter unplugged.
  5. Close the case, zero the ohmmeter on each range against a known resistor, and confirm readings before returning the tester to service.

⚠ Unlike the two-piece version, this board carries real mains voltage right next to the regulators — and shares an enclosure with test leads you'll clip onto live industrial circuits. Give the module's L/N/fuse/switch wiring real physical clearance from everything else (several mm minimum, more if you can), sleeve every mains-side joint, and don't skip the chassis ground bond. If in doubt, the two-piece version with a separate enclosed converter keeps mains wiring further from the regulators and is the more forgiving build.
